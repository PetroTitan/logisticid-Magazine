Freight is an industry where the most useful sentence is often "it depends", and where almost everything published for a general audience says something more confident than that. Search for how long a pallet takes to move from Rotterdam to Prague and you will find a number. The number will not tell you which carrier, which equipment, which season, which border, or whether the load can be consolidated — and every one of those changes the answer.

LogisticID Magazine exists to write the version that does not skip those. It is published by LogisticID, a freight forwarder arranging European road freight through third-party transport providers, and it is written for shippers, carriers, and anyone whose job has recently started involving a truck.

## What this publication covers

Three things, to begin with.

**How road freight works.** What actually distinguishes a full truckload from a part load, what equipment a shipment needs, what has to be agreed before a vehicle is booked, and which documents exist because someone downstream cannot proceed without them.

**How to work with a forwarder.** What information makes a quote request answerable, how to describe cargo so the description survives contact with a loading bay, and where planning most reliably goes wrong.

**How to read freight claims.** Market figures, transit times and efficiency claims circulate in this industry with their methodology stripped off. A recurring theme here is how to tell a measured number from a repeated one.

Sections are added when there is published work to put in them, not in advance. An empty section is a promise of coverage that does not exist, and this publication would rather have three honest sections than nine aspirational ones.

## The rules this publication works under

These are worth stating plainly, because they are the reason to trust anything below a headline.

**Nothing is invented to fill a page.** No freight rate, surcharge, transit time, availability, customs requirement, sanctions conclusion, carrier eligibility decision, quotation, statistic, emissions figure or citation appears here unless it has been verified against a real source. Where a fact has not been supplied and confirmed, the page says nothing rather than estimating.

**Sources are named and checkable.** Material factual claims carry a numbered reference to a source you can open. The build refuses to publish an article that cites a source it has not listed — and equally refuses one that lists a source it never cites, because a bibliography of unused authorities borrows credibility without doing any work.

**The commercial interest is disclosed rather than disguised.** LogisticID benefits from readers who understand freight well enough to work with a forwarder. So there is no advertising, no affiliate arrangement, no paid placement, no carrier marketplace and no ranking of competitors. Links to LogisticID service pages appear after an article, labelled, not woven into its argument.

**There are no popularity rankings.** Nothing here is sorted by "trending", "most read" or "recommended". Those require first-party data and a stated methodology. Without both they are decoration that reads as evidence, and this publication has neither yet.

## Where this publication stops

:::boundary
LogisticID Magazine is general information about how freight works. It is not an assessment of your shipment. It does not replace a forwarder's shipment-specific advice, a carrier's acceptance decision, a customs broker or customs authority, legal, tax, sanctions or insurance advice, a dangerous-goods safety adviser, or review of the contract you are actually signing.
:::

That boundary is not a disclaimer bolted onto the end of articles. It changes how they are written. Where an outcome genuinely depends on route, cargo, equipment, contract, jurisdiction, date or a carrier's acceptance, the article says so instead of producing a number that would be more satisfying and less true.

## Corrections

Articles get things wrong. When a material point is wrong here, the article is corrected, a dated note explaining what changed is attached to it, and it is listed on the [corrections page](/corrections) — with the original claim described rather than quietly deleted, so a reader who acted on the earlier version can tell whether it affected them.

The full detail is in the [editorial policy](/editorial-policy), the [sourcing policy](/sourcing-policy) and the [image and AI policy](/image-policy). They are linked from the footer of every page, which is where a reader is most likely to want them.
